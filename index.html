      Case study · NeurIPS 2026 Competition Track

      Agenthon 2026 Track 2: 1st of 87 teams

      • Team Sitadel Insecurities
      • Sep – Oct 2026
      • Python · NumPy · pandas · Docker

      The challenge

      Each “forecast card” holds dated data panels, a text corpus and an as-of date. The agent outputs thousands of joint predictive draws for every target: U.S. Treasury yields from 3 months to 30 years, G10 and emerging-market exchange rates, and the next print of monthly statistics such as CPI and PCE. Entries are scored on a CRPS, variogram and tail-quantile composite, normalized against a published random-walk baseline, on a sealed final week (forecasts made 26 Oct 2026 for 27–30 Oct).

      Approach

      • Volatility that adapts. Variance starts from a blend of EWMA and long-run variance, then mean-reverts. Each draw is one joint path for every asset and horizon.
      • Tails from the data, not a Gaussian. Shocks are whole-date rows of vol-standardized residuals, so fat tails and cross-asset dependence come from history; one volatility-regime multiplier per draw is shared by all assets.
      • Event-aware. FOMC, ECB and Bank of Japan decision days get variance multipliers fitted on 25 years of data; near an FOMC decision, part of the draws borrow residuals from past decision weeks.
      • Nowcasts for data releases. Bridge regressions (e.g. PCE from the CPI already on the card), a robust AR(1) with Huber month effects, and base-effect forecasts for annual rates.
      • Can't crash. Every output is checked (finite values, spreads within a band of the baseline's); failures fall back to the baseline, then a random walk.

      Evidence before submitting

      We built our own local leaderboard: thousands of rolling-origin backtest cards from public history, scored with the official scoring code. Head-to-head on 4,752 final-like daily cards during development (normalized score, lower is better):

      MethodScore
      Our engine1.098
      GARCH-t + event calendar (best rival)1.104
      GJR-GARCH-t + event calendar1.105
      Filtered historical simulation + calendar1.108
      HAR + event calendar1.113

      On monthly statistic cards the gap is wider: CPI-index cards scored 0.852 vs AutoARIMA's 0.988 and the baseline's 1.800.

      Engineering

      Forecasts are deterministic across time zone, locale, hash seed and thread count, with no network access or wall-clock reads. The submitted Docker image is public and byte-identical to the repository (SHA-256 checksums), and a provenance file documents where every constant came from and why it was available before the forecast date.

      Case study · NeurIPS 2026 Competition Track

      Agenthon 2026 Track 4: 4th of 63 teams

      • Team Sitadel Insecurities
      • Sep – Oct 2026
      • Python · LLM agents · Docker

      The challenge

      Each unit gives a task (a roster of companies or series, a target and a cutoff date) plus an embargo-filtered corpus of documents. For every entity the agent predicts a label, a point forecast and a 90% interval, then justifies them with claims and reasons that quote the corpus verbatim. A pinned NLI model judges whether each cited claim is actually supported.

      Approach

      • Floor first. A schema-checked fallback answer is written before any network call. The LLM's answer replaces it only if it passes the same checks, so a unit never scores as failed.
      • Retrieval from scratch. BM25 over chunked documents in pure standard-library Python, plus verbatim sentence spans for citations.
      • Budgeted LLM reads. Several reads per batch from the competition's House model under a hard 23-of-25 request budget and a per-unit deadline, with JSON repair and no blind retries.
      • Statistics + LLM. Where a target has history, a rolling-origin backtest picks the best simple forecaster; the LLM's median is blended 50/50 with it, and intervals are widened ×1.4 for known LLM under-coverage.
      • Self-verification. Before writing, the agent runs the official scorer's checks and drops any claim that would fail.

      How we iterated

      11 versions in 12 days, each change A/B-tested on practice units. Some ideas were rejected on evidence: “thinking mode” gave no score gain and truncated replies on 6 of 11 units, so it shipped switched off.

      Case study

      Payment Fraud Detection Pipeline

      • Personal project
      • 2026
      • XGBoost · LightGBM · SHAP · MLflow · ONNX

      The problem

      Card fraud is rare (3.5% of the 590K IEEE-CIS transactions) and has to be caught in milliseconds. The goal is a production-shaped system, not a notebook model.

      What's built

      • Feature pipeline: merges transaction and identity tables, drops columns more than 90% missing, derives time-of-day and day-of-week, normalizes email domains, writes Parquet.
      • Models: XGBoost and LightGBM with class weighting, plus a weighted ensemble; F1-optimal thresholds saved for serving.
      • MLOps: every run logged to MLflow with versioned models and a Staging alias; models exported to ONNX for fast CPU inference.

      Catching the leak

      The first results showed AUC 0.96–0.97, above what heavily tuned winning Kaggle solutions reach on this dataset (~0.945–0.96), so I treated it as a red flag rather than a win. Two leaks were inflating it:

      • Random split on time-ordered data. Shuffling before splitting let the model train on future transactions and validate on past ones. Fixed with a chronological split: oldest 80% to train, most recent 20% to validate.
      • Imputation leakage. Missing values were filled with medians computed on the full dataset, including validation rows. Fixed by fitting the imputer on the training set only.

      The honest result: AUC 0.91 (ensemble 0.912), about 0.05 lower, which is exactly the leakage that had been inflating it.

      The early-stopping bug

      LightGBM's first run stopped after a single boosting round. With no metric set, early stopping watched log-loss, which gets worse on an imbalanced validation set as fraud is up-weighted, even while ranking quality is still improving. Switching to scale_pos_weight (matching XGBoost) and AUC as the stopping metric let it train fully.
